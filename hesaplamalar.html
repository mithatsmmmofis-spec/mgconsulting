Period parameters

Maintained by the firm and updated when the legislation changes. Visitors cannot alter them; every calculation below uses exactly these values.

Severance and notice pay

Notice periods are those set in the Labour Law; the severance ceiling comes from the panel.

Regular, recurring benefits are added to the severance base. Enter monthly amounts; leave blank if none. One-off items such as overtime or performance bonuses are not added.

Severance is subject to stamp duty only. Notice pay is subject to income tax and stamp duty; the income tax shown uses the top band entered and should be checked against the cumulative base.

Default surcharge

Months are charged in full and part months by day, as the collection law provides.

Uses the monthly rate from the panel. Where the rate changed during the period, run each sub-period separately.

Deadline calculator

Adds the statutory period and carries a weekend ending to the next working day.

Periods run from the day after notification. Confirm the holiday list for the year; it is not pre-filled.

Depreciation

Straight line, declining balance and pro rata in the year of acquisition.

Declining balance uses twice the straight-line rate, capped at 50 per cent. Pro rata applies to passenger cars.

Cash conversion cycle

Receivable, inventory and payable days, and the working capital they tie up.

Working capital requirement is the cycle expressed in days applied to daily cost of sales.

Passenger car expense restriction

Splits running costs into the deductible and non-deductible portions, with the VAT effect.

Seventy per cent is deductible. Whether the VAT on the non-deductible portion may be recovered is contested; it is shown separately here.

Basis. Rates and periods set directly in legislation are applied as such. Amounts indexed annually are taken only from the parameters panel; nothing is assumed.

Use. These are working calculations for professional use, not an assessment of any specific matter. Results are checked against the legislation in force before they are relied on.
